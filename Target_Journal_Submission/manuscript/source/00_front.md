# Robust time-series classification under sensor dropouts: observed-tap-weighted MiniRocket and a pre-specified evaluation

**Abstract**

Sensors in deployed monitoring systems drop out: wireless nodes lose packets, acquisition units disconnect and instruments saturate. Classifiers trained on clean recordings must nevertheless remain reliable. Random-convolution classifiers such as MiniRocket are fast and accurate but defined only for complete series, so gaps are usually interpolated first. Writing MiniRocket's zero-sum kernel response as a contrast of tap-group means shows why a native estimator from observed values is unbiased but noisy, and why interpolation fabricates responses inside long gaps. We propose MissRocket, which keeps interpolated responses but weights each response's contribution to pooling by the fraction of its taps that were observed. It adds no learned parameters and reduces to MiniRocket on complete data. In a pre-specified evaluation on {{N_CONF}} UCR datasets with three missingness mechanisms at three rates, MissRocket improved on MiniRocket with interpolation by {{Q:table_pooled_statistics;baseline=MiniRocket + linear interp.;col=mean_diff;fmt=pp}} percentage points on average (Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon p {{Q:table_pooled_statistics;baseline=MiniRocket + linear interp.;col=p_holm;fmt=p}}), by up to {{Q:table_main_statistics;mechanism=block;rate=0.8;baseline=MiniRocket + linear interp.;col=mean_diff;fmt=pp}} points for contiguous gaps, and not significantly under random missingness. In a bearing-fault case study (CWRU), models trained on complete data and tested with long sensor outages kept {{Q:table_e9_summary;condition=shift_outage_long30;method=MissRocket;col=accuracy;fmt=f3}} accuracy with MissRocket ({{Q:table_e9_summary;condition=shift_outage_long30;method=MaskRocket-noC;col=accuracy;fmt=f3}} with the native estimator), whereas interpolation-based MiniRocket fell to {{Q:table_e9_summary;condition=shift_outage_long30;method=AeonMiniRocket+linear;col=accuracy;fmt=f3}}, mostly by confusing fault types. With the same outages in the training data, missing-value handling mattered little and Hydra-MultiRocket was most accurate. Cross-series kNN imputation was more accurate for long gaps on UCR, and on sparse clinical records (PhysioNet 2012) GRU-D and gradient boosting outperformed all random-convolution methods.

# Response to the external (pre-submission) review

The reviewer recommended a major revision. Their main requests were a real-world industrial application, an industrial
vibration experiment with sensor dropouts, a decision-rule table, a new title and research question, a sensor-reliability
framing of the abstract, a clearer statement of novelty, reconciled runtime claims, the P12 result kept as a boundary of
applicability, an explanation of the excluded datasets, removal of placeholders, and accurate pre-specification and
AI-use statements. Each point is answered below, with where the change appears in the manuscript. All new numbers are
generated by the code from the raw results (`code/results/full/E9_cwru/raw_results.csv`, `tables/table_e9_*.csv`,
`tables/table_edge.csv`).

| # | Reviewer request | Response | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Real-world industrial application story (sensor dropouts, predictive maintenance) | The Introduction now opens with condition monitoring and sensor dropouts (wireless packet loss, acquisition disconnects, out-of-range values). The Discussion adds a deployment perspective for predictive maintenance. | Sect. 1; Sect. 7.3 |
| 2 | Industrial vibration experiment on CWRU: short/medium/long outages; complete vs random 10/30/50%; contiguous outages; train complete → test incomplete; identical masks | New experiment E9 (CWRU 12 kHz drive-end, 10 classes, four loads, leave-one-load-out folds, 2,399 segments of 1,024 samples). It covers 13 scenarios: complete; random 10/30/50%; short/medium/long outages at 30%; long outages at 50%; and the same dropout scenarios with complete training data. Masks are hashed per fold, scenario, seed and split, so every method sees identical corrupted data. The design was committed before any E9 run; the one exception (a timing test) is disclosed. | Sect. 5.1–5.3, 5.5 (deviation 4), 6.10; Online Resource 1 S15; Online Resource 3 |
| 3 | Metrics: accuracy, macro-F1, balanced accuracy, per-class recall, degradation vs complete, inference time and peak memory on an edge CPU | All are reported. Inference cost is measured on one CPU thread in a fresh process as an edge-CPU proxy. The paper states that this is not a measurement on embedded hardware. | Sect. 6.10, @tab:cwru, @tab:edge; S15–S16 |
| 4 | Compare MiniRocket, MissRocket, Hydra-MultiRocket and missing-aware baselines | E9 compares MissRocket, its γ=0 ablation, aeon MiniRocket with linear interpolation, aeon MiniRocket with kNN imputation, Hydra-MultiRocket with interpolation, the native masked estimator and LightGBM with native NaN handling.
